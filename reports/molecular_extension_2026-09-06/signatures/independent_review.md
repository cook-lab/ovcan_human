# Independent scientific review of the SBS3 extension

Reviewed 6 September 2026. Scope: script 44, the companion figure script/legend, Methods, source manifest, count matrices and summary/uncertainty tables. This was a code/design and result-consistency review, not an independent rerun of the 36,800 bootstrap fits. No analysis files were modified.

**No material issue identified.** The analysis is appropriate as an exploratory assessment of SBS3 attribution sensitivity. The conclusions remain correctly limited: no validated HRD class, HR proficiency, somatic burden or present repair function is inferred.

The population screens explicitly start from MAF PASS, preserving converter-added filtering. Missing population annotations remain eligible and are quantified, so the stricter frequency threshold is not misrepresented as proof of somatic origin. Avoiding a generic heterozygous/high-VAF exclusion is appropriate for pure and aneuploid cultures. The read screen changes the candidate mixture as well as its size; it is a sensitivity condition, not a truth set. These limitations are already stated in Methods.

Target-restricted variants are compared under both genome and target-adjusted dictionaries. The target/genome trinucleotide transformation has the correct direction and normalization, is checked against the deposited reference, and uses a verified target union. The genome-reference fits are useful controls for the effect of a mismatched opportunity assumption; they should not be treated as equally well-matched estimates of the actual capture process. Neither adjustment incorporates sample-specific callability, as Methods correctly notes.

Full and restricted dictionaries are presented as alternative modelling assumptions. SBS3 is similar to other broad spectra: the target-adjusted SBS3–SBS40 reference cosine is approximately 0.894. Dictionary restriction can therefore make apparent SBS3 support increase without adding new biological evidence. Strict backward selection, component-resampling diagnostics and the independent NNLS removal diagnostic expose this problem rather than conceal it. The approach is consistent with the purpose of strict refitting and robustness assessment in the [primary MutationalPatterns methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8845394/).

Uncertainty is correctly conditioned on the retained candidate spectrum and reference model. Bootstrap fractions use each replicate's fitted denominator; never-selected components are zero-filled. The shared sample/screen seeds support paired dictionary/opportunity contrasts. Selection fractions are not interpreted as posterior probabilities, and tiny NNLS residual differences are not treated as hypothesis tests. Machine-precision negative residual differences around 10^-19 should simply be read as numerical zero. Two hundred resamples are adequate for this descriptive instability check; the tails should not be interpreted as highly precise diagnostic limits.

Related samples are retained for model comparison, while descriptive patient summaries average within the 16 patient groups. They are not used as 23 independent patients, nor are overlapping mutations in related isolates presented as independent validation. A mean of model-level fitted fractions is a descriptive summary, not a reconstructed ancestral patient spectrum; the existing column name makes that distinction clear.

Independent consistency checks reproduced the reported candidate totals and distributions: baseline 12,984 SNVs, range 303–2,417, median 430; AF-only 10,548, range 221–2,129, median 340; combined screen 6,738, range 127–1,668, median 216, with eight models below 200. All files in the source manifest matched their saved SHA-256 hashes.

The strongest biological conclusion is the instability itself. For target-adjusted full dictionaries, only two baseline and six combined-screen point fits retain SBS3, and **all 23 corresponding bootstrap percentile ranges include zero under each screen**. Genome-restricted fits retain SBS3 in 21 models under each screen, with positive lower percentiles in 12 and 11 models respectively. This divergence supports the existing decision not to assign HRD/HR-proficient labels. TOV81D's pathogenic BRCA2 allele and the patient-3133 CDK12 observations remain separate genetic evidence; neither should be validated or contradicted solely by these conditional SBS3 fits.

The decision not to substitute a sparse-data classifier without checking its training, assay and input compatibility is also justified. [SigMA's primary validation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-019-0390-2) does not by itself calibrate probabilities for these filtered tumour-only cell-model spectra. The documented next steps—appropriate allele counts/copy-state analysis and functional or orthogonal evidence where required—remain proportionate to the intended model annotations.
