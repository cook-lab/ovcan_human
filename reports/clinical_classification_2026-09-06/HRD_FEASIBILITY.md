# HRD-related classification: evidence and feasible next analyses

**Assessment: 6 September 2026.** This review uses the corrected v9 WES/CNV analysis. It does not change candidate tiers, copy-number calls, signature fits or manuscript conclusions. No HRD package was installed and no new alignment, variant-calling or allele-specific fit was run.

**A defensible HRD analysis is possible in stages, but the current relative CNV tables cannot yield an HRD scar score directly.** The most suitable next computational route is tumour-only allele-specific copy-number fitting with PureCN, followed by exploratory scarHRD metrics if the fit passes quality checks. The immediate obstacle is the retained SNP input, not an inherent inability to study HRD with WES. Separately, an exact BRCA2 pathogenic allele can already be annotated in TOV81D; its presence does not establish loss of the other allele or current functional HR deficiency.

## Keep the classification dimensions separate

| Dimension | Appropriate description | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| HRR-gene genotype | Exact variant, clinical classification and review authority; somatic/germline origin and allelic state in separate fields | Several candidates; one expert-panel BRCA2 allele match verified below |
| Genomic scars | LOH, telomeric allelic imbalance (TAI), large-scale transitions (LST), and their sum from a valid allele-specific solution | Not computed; major/minor CN and ploidy are unavailable |
| Mutational process | SBS3 or other HR-associated mutation patterns with fitting uncertainty | Exploratory refits exist; these are not a validated HRD classifier |
| Current repair function | Damage-induced RAD51 foci in an appropriately cycling, damage-positive population, or another validated functional assay | No stock-linked result established by this review |
| Drug response | Measured response under a specified drug, dose, schedule and assay | Not inferred from genotype, scars, expression or FGA |

The original scarHRD study demonstrated transfer of scar measures from SNP arrays to WES in breast cancers, supporting technical feasibility rather than a clinical cutoff for these cell lines. Its input includes total and allele-specific segment copy numbers and ploidy. FGA, arm-level relative gains/losses, or a transition count alone cannot substitute for this input. [scarHRD paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41523-018-0066-6), [author input specification](https://github.com/sztup/scarHRD).

For example, the myChoice CDx documentation uses deleterious/suspected-deleterious BRCA status or GIS ≥42 in its specified clinical testing framework. Applying 42 to an open-source scar sum from cultured models would not reproduce that assay's analytical or clinical validation. Report exploratory continuous components and quality status before considering any independently justified threshold. [FDA technical documentation](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf19/P190014S003C.pdf).

## The decisive SNP-input check

The reproducible read-only probe `hrd_input_probe.py` verifies every VCF against the saved hash and writes `hrd_vcf_input_counts.csv` and `hrr_candidate_vcf_evidence.csv` beside this report. Its counts are record diagnostics, not inferred germline genotypes.

All 23 actual Mutect2 command headers record `--genotype-germline-sites false`, `--genotype-pon-sites false` and `--interval-padding 0`. This differs from PureCN's documented germline-inclusive input requirements. Among autosomal biallelic SNVs with exactly two AD counts and total AD ≥20, the all-status VCFs contain **4,622–6,527 common sites per model**, defined by `POPAF < 2` (the VCF's negative-log10 population AF, therefore AF >0.01). Restricting to VCF PASS leaves **26–60**. There are also 1,435–6,126 sites/model with raw alternate AD fraction 0.3–0.7, but that fraction alone does not establish a germline heterozygote in an aneuploid cell line.

**Do not feed the PASS-only VCF, MAF PASS subset or 6,194 coding candidates into an allele-specific fit as a germline SNP set.** Even the all-status archive cannot be certified as unbiased SNP input because upstream calling skipped sites according to germline/PoN evidence. The documented PureCN route requires both genotype flags enabled and recommends caller interval padding. [PureCN best practices, §3](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/vignettes/PureCN/inst/doc/Quick.html).

Local R inspection found `VariantAnnotation` available, but neither `PureCN` nor `scarHRD` installed. A pilot was therefore not launched: installing software would still leave the substantive input-eligibility problem unresolved.

## Method selection

| Method | Fit to these data | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| PureCN | Designed for targeted/exome tumour-only data, including pure cell lines; combines ratios and allelic counts to compare purity/ploidy solutions | First exploratory route after SNP-input recovery. Existing corrected CNVkit inputs can avoid repeating coverage/segmentation from scratch |
| scarHRD | Downstream scorer of valid allele-specific segments; not an allele-specific caller | Compute LOH/TAI/LST and sum only after fit acceptance; retain failed/ambiguous models as unassessed |
| Sequenza / FACETS | Standard workflows use matched tumour–normal allelic evidence | Appropriate if genuine patient normals are found. Five unrelated reference exomes are not interchangeable with patient-matched germline genotypes |
| HRDetect | Original model uses WGS features, including rearrangements. The original paper also evaluated a separately retrained WES model with reduced sensitivity | Do not label it categorically WGS-only; do not run the standard WGS classifier with missing features filled with zero. A specific WES implementation and its validation would be a separate project |
| CHORD | Uses somatic SNVs, indels and structural variants, including microhomology deletions and tandem-duplication patterns | The present exomes lack the required reliable genome-wide SV catalogue; not a suitable immediate route |
| SBS3 refitting | Available SNV spectra can give supporting evidence, with restricted substrate and model dependence | Keep exploratory, with uncertainty and capture-opportunity sensitivity; do not convert exposure >0 to HRD-positive |

PureCN's original method explicitly supports unmatched high-purity samples; its later tumour-only WES benchmark included ovarian cancers. Neither establishes a commercial HRD diagnosis in these particular stocks. [PureCN method](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5157099/), [WES validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259876/). Standard paired designs are described by [Sequenza](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25319062/) and [FACETS](https://github.com/mskcc/facets). The original HRDetect study reported 46.8% sensitivity when its WGS model was applied to WES, improving to 73% after WES-specific retraining, with additional positive classifications relative to the WGS model. [HRDetect](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5833945/). [CHORD author documentation](https://github.com/UMCUGenetics/CHORD) specifies the mutation and SV features.

Existing `output/wes_signature_refit_exposures.csv` provides a concrete uncertainty example: OV1369-R2 has an unadjusted full-reference SBS3 fraction 0.377, but its 200-bootstrap 95% contribution interval includes zero (0–324.8 mutations), and selection frequency is 0.725. The current target-opportunity sensitivity summary concentrates on MMR fractions; it is not an independently validated SBS3 classifier. A future HRD-focused signature analysis should audit germline residuals, compare reference restrictions and target-normalized fits, and retain uncertainty rather than borrowing this point estimate as a scar score.

## HRR variants that merit immediate annotation or validation

The probe checks the eight-gene set BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, RAD51C, RAD51D, BRIP1, BARD1 and CDK12 in the retained coding table. It finds 13 rows in BRCA2, PALB2 and CDK12. Absence of a retained candidate in another gene is not evidence of normal function; deletions, unretained variants, methylation and other mechanisms are not excluded.

**TOV81D BRCA2 is a verified pathogenic-allele match.** The VCF `chr13:32371000 AAG>A` corresponds to MAF GRCh38 `32371001–32371002 AG>-`, matching ClinVar rs80359714 / VCV000009328.54. ClinVar classifies it as germline **Pathogenic**, reviewed by the ENIGMA expert panel, SCV000301287.2 (evaluation 2016-09-08). Its MANE name is **NM_000059.4:c.8537_8538del, p.Glu2846fs**; the full consequence is reported as p.Glu2846GlyfsTer22. The local `p.R2845fs` is a reconstructed fallback, not this normalized HGVS label. The repeat-region identity is supported by the exact deleted coordinates, alleles and rs identifier, not protein-name similarity. [ClinVar record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/variation/9328/).

Source VCF support is AD **75 reference / 45 alternate**, raw fraction **0.375**, caller AF **0.392**, TLOD **121.63**, FILTER PASS. The MAF's missing indel VAF is therefore a recoverable omission. A separate `curated_variant_classifications.csv` records this evidence without altering the original Tier3 table. Germline pathogenicity is the reference record's classification; **origin in this model, biallelic inactivation, current HR function and drug response remain distinct questions**. Historical TOV-81D work reported both mutant and wild-type BRCA2 transcripts, further cautioning against a BRCA2-null label. [Allelic-transcript study](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerpreventionresearch/article/5/5/765/32041/Allelic-Transcripts-Dosage-Effect-in). Prioritize current-stock BRCA2 locus allelic fitting and orthogonal allele confirmation, including the separately unresolved provider pool alias.

TOV3133D's BRCA2 `chr13:32316525 C>A` (current p.A22E) has only **5 alternate / 124 total AD**, caller AF **0.038** and TLOD **5.10**. No exact expert-panel pathogenicity assertion was established in this review. Do not turn a low-fraction missense candidate into a BRCA2-deficient classification or transfer an assertion for a different alternate allele at that site.

The CDK12 `chr17:39462437 AC>A` frameshift candidate (current p.L123fs) has **zero reference / 36–42 alternate AD** across four related 3133 sublines. This supports allelic predominance and a shared patient-family lead, not four independent cases or proof of biallelic inactivation. Check normalization, transcript consequence, read alignment and allele-specific copy state before assigning CDK12 loss. Ovarian cancers with inactivating CDK12 mutations can have a tandem-duplication phenotype distinct from BRCA1/2-associated scars; do not label every HRR-gene alteration equivalent HRD. [Primary CDK12 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26787835/). Other PALB2/CDK12 candidates and their low-frequency read evidence are retained in the companion CSV for curation. Gene-specific expert guidance should inform pathogenicity assessment; the project's heuristic priority tier is not an ACMG/AMP class. [ClinGen ENIGMA specifications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393667/).

## Functional and publication interpretation

Genomic scars record past damage and can persist after repair restoration. A functional RAD51 assay asks whether the current stock can recruit RAD51 after damage; cell-cycle and damage controls are necessary to distinguish deficient HR from a noncycling or inadequately challenged culture. RAD51 transcript/protein abundance in the supplied omics matrices is not that assay. Primary studies show the distinction between scars and restored repair/resistance, and functional testing has been studied in ovarian material. [RAD51 and resistance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5961353/), [ovarian functional assay](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/24/18/4482/81005/A-Functional-Homologous-Recombination-Assay).

For this Data Descriptor, useful new fields can be `HRR_variant_classification`, `allelic_state`, `scar_score_status`, `functional_HR_status` and `evidence_source`, allowing unknown/inconclusive values. Expression-associated HRD signatures may be studied as transcriptional associations, but should not replace allele-specific scars or functional evidence. Neither “BRCA wild-type”, “HR-proficient” nor “PARP-sensitive” follows from a missing retained candidate or a single genomic metric. These additional classifications can enrich model selection without becoming a prerequisite for publishing the already supported resource.
