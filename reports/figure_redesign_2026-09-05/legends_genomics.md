# Genomic figure legends after the September 2026 redesign

## Figure 4. Variant curation, external expression matching and copy-number profiles

**A**, Variant counts before and after filtering, shown for all 23 models with whole-exome sequencing (WES) and for OV2295 as an example. The logarithmic count axis shows all Mutect2 records, calls passing FilterMutectCalls, and coding nonsynonymous candidates retained after the specified population-frequency filter (maximum annotated frequency ≤0.001). Across the 23 models, the corresponding counts are 582,474, 16,081 and 6,194; OV2295 contributes 25,914, 493 and 170. These are tumour-only candidates, not confirmed somatic mutations.

**B**, Expression matching for the five models shared with DepMap Public 24Q4. Correlations use 2,000 variable genes and all 67 DepMap ovarian models. Grey ticks show the 66 non-self correlations per project model; the open rust circle marks the best non-self match and the filled circle the self-match. Each self-match ranks first among the 67 comparisons and is the reciprocal best match among the project RNA models. BIN67 and COV434 are each other's closest non-self matches. These comparisons support identity for this overlapping subset and do not authenticate every model or replace current-stock STR testing.

**C**, Canonical-driver candidates in 23 models from 16 patients. Columns are grouped by histotype and patient. HGS, high-grade serous; CC, clear cell; EC, endometrioid; LGS, low-grade serous; MC, mucinous. These are the recorded histotype annotations; discordant evidence for individual models is discussed separately. Brackets identify the four patient groups with multiple WES-profiled models in this panel. The upper track shows each model's coding-candidate count on a logarithmic scale, with a lower reference of 100. Each gene/model marker combines variant class (square, truncating; circle, missense; triangle, in-frame indel; diamond, multiple variant classes) with the best priority tier present in that cell. Tier 1 is the strongest prioritization category, followed by Tiers 2 and 3; the tiers do not establish somatic origin. Pale empty cells indicate no retained candidate in the displayed gene. The right-hand bars and labels count patients with at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 candidate, using the union across their derived models and a denominator of 16. Tier 3 candidates remain visible but are excluded from these patient counts. Genes are ordered by the Tier 1–2 patient count. TP53 candidates are retained in all 18 HGSC models, representing 11 patients.

**D**, Gain and loss frequencies across 39 assessed autosomal arms in the 11 HGSC patients. Gains extend upward and losses downward; axis labels show absolute percentages. A model-level arm is called altered when more than half of the full non-centromeric arm length has centered log2 copy ratio above 0.20 or below −0.20. Segments are intersected with hg38 cytoband-defined arms, excluding centromeric intervals. Acrocentric p arms 13p, 14p, 15p, 21p and 22p are not assessed. Patient calls are the union across derived models, so a patient can contribute to both directions if different derived models meet different criteria. CNVkit log2 ratios are centered by the autosomal probe-weighted median. These relative ratios do not determine absolute ploidy, loss of heterozygosity or homologous-recombination deficiency.

## Figure 5. Exploratory mutation spectra and selected model-specific molecular profiles

**A**, Coding-candidate counts across all 23 WES-profiled models, ordered by count. The horizontal count axis is logarithmic; point colour indicates the proportion of coding SNVs and indels that are indels. The dashed line marks the median of 206 candidates. TOV21G has 1,416 coding candidates, approximately 6.9 times the median. This within-panel outlier is not a calibrated tumour mutational burden measurement.

**B**, The SBS-96 spectrum of TOV21G, derived from 2,417 exome-wide SNVs passing the caller and population-frequency filters. Channels are grouped by the six pyrimidine-centered substitution classes and ordered by the 5′ and 3′ flanking bases within each class. Bar heights are fractions of the model's SNVs. The spectrum uses a broader candidate set than the coding-only counts in A and the GRCh38 reference sequence.

**C**, Maximum cosine similarity between the TOV21G spectrum and the prespecified mismatch-repair-associated, clock-like and POLE-proofreading-associated groups of COSMIC v3.2 SBS signatures. The displayed labels identify each group's strongest match: SBS6, 0.877; SBS5, 0.651; and SBS10c, 0.315. These are descriptive similarities without a diagnostic cutoff; similarity to a reference signature alone does not establish a mutational process or MSI status.

**D**, Sensitivity of the TOV21G MMR-associated fitted fraction to the reference setting. All 2,417 SNVs overlap the recovered CNVkit target footprint. “Genome / full” uses the genome-based reference with the full signature set; “Target / full” adjusts reference trinucleotide opportunities to that target footprint; “Target / restricted” uses the adjusted reference and the prespecified restricted signature set. The resulting fractions are 0.733, 0.447 and 0.475. These are exploratory point estimates, with no confidence intervals shown. The material change after opportunity adjustment and the unresolved somatic/germline mixture limit biological interpretation. MSI status remains unconfirmed.

**E**, RNA and protein expression of selected lineage markers in the three models annotated as mucinous. Markers comprise KRT7, PAX8 and WT1; intestinal-associated SATB2, CDX2, KRT20 and MUC2; and gastric-associated MUC5AC, TFF1 and TFF3. Each gene is standardized across all 31 models measured in its assay, rather than across only the three displayed models. Grey cells with crosses indicate markers not measured in that assay. Expression patterns are descriptive and do not independently determine ovarian versus gastrointestinal origin.

**F**, Selected SWI/SNF-related molecular evidence. The six models satisfying the exploratory subunit-loss rules are shown together with OV2295, whose Tier-3-only truncating candidate is not admitted as loss evidence. Fill shows the within-assay gene z score; numbers give abundance rank among the 31 models in that assay, with 1 indicating the lowest value. Compact WES labels give the affected gene and its best priority tier. An asterisk marks a truncating candidate excluded from the operational loss rule because its only support is Tier 3. An empty WES label is not a claim of wild-type status or absence of WES. In particular, BIN67 has SMARCA4 RNA rank 23/31 and protein rank 2/31, illustrating RNA/protein discordance. Relative abundance and these exploratory rules do not establish complete protein loss or a histopathological reclassification. E and F share a slate–white–rust z-score scale displayed with saturation at ±3; untruncated values remain in the source tables.

## Supplementary Figure S5. Genome-wide relative copy-number profiles

Rows show all 23 WES-profiled models; columns show consecutive 10-Mb bins across chromosomes 1–22 in GRCh38 (300 bins, with shorter terminal bins). Each displayed value is the segment-overlap-length-weighted mean of the centered CNVkit log2 copy ratios intersecting that bin. Histotype labels identify the row groups. The patient-family strip identifies related models using the same family identifiers as the main figures; “Other” denotes models outside those named multi-model families. The heatmap has no row or column clustering.

The slate–white–rust scale is centered at zero and saturates at ±1.5. Grey cells indicate no segment overlap, not absence of WES or demonstrated absence of exome coverage. The right-hand bars show fraction of genome altered (FGA), calculated from the underlying autosomal segments rather than from the display bins: the fraction of represented autosomal segment length with absolute centered log2 ratio above 0.20. TOV81D has the lowest FGA (0.021). Profiles are centered by the autosomal probe-weighted median; sex chromosomes are excluded. The external reference normals used the same capture kit according to the author. Relative profiles and FGA do not establish absolute ploidy, allele-specific copy number or loss of heterozygosity. The coarser display bins should not be used to infer individual-gene copy status.

## Supplementary Figure S6. Exploratory classifier outputs and RNA pathway-score clusters

**A**, Uncalibrated class probabilities from consensusOV 1.30.0 for the 15 HGSC RNA-profiled models. Columns show differentiated (DIF), proliferative (PRO), immunoreactive (IMR) and mesenchymal (MES) classes. Cell labels give the probabilities rounded to two decimals; an outline identifies the highest-probability class. The model ordering follows this class and the top-minus-second probability margin. The asterisk marks OV2085, whose assignment changes from IMR to PRO when the classifier is applied using the broader RNA-model input set instead of the HGSC-only set.

**B**, Top-minus-second probability margins, with rows aligned to A. The pale shaded region marks margins below 0.10 (six models); the dashed line marks the median margin of 0.158. These are descriptive summaries of classifier ambiguity, not calibrated confidence or significance thresholds.

**C**, Illustrative k=3 Ward.D2 clusters from the HGSC Hallmark pathway scores, using the same model ordering. Cluster 1 is the hypoxic/glycolytic group, cluster 2 the low-signalling group and cluster 3 the inflammatory/NF-κB/EMT group. These names summarize relative pathway patterns within this panel. Assignments change when one model per patient is selected and the clustering is repeated (15 models, 12 patients; adjusted Rand index 0.189 on shared representatives). Bulk-tumour classifier classes can reflect microenvironmental contributions and are not validated histotypes for these cultures. Neither the classifier outputs nor these unstable RNA clusters establish model identity or new biological subtypes.
