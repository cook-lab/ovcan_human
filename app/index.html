How to read this & what to watch for

  • Bars are relative by default. Length is scaled to the highest line for that gene (RNA on a log10 basis; protein on its linear log2 scale). Switch to Shared axis to keep each assay's display range fixed across genes. Protein feature baselines are not calibrated for comparisons of absolute abundance between proteins. Printed numbers retain the supplied units.
  • RNA vs protein are different assays and units — TPM (within-sample-normalised transcript abundance) vs TMT log2 protein abundance. Compare them by rank/pattern, not by matching numbers. Protein differences can reflect both post-transcriptional biology and TMT measurement effects.
  • "n.d." = no quantified value in that assay. Protein has structural block-missingness (~9% of values): a protein absent from a whole TMT plex is missing for every line in that plex, not necessarily biologically absent. Absence of protein is weaker evidence than presence.
  • Histotype, source site, and TMT plex are partly confounded. Several histotype-by-centre combinations are not represented. A difference that tracks subtype may partly reflect batch or site. Models from the same patient are not independent biological replicates.
  • Gene identifiers: RNA is keyed by Ensembl gene ID (release 93) and mapped to symbols; where several Ensembl genes share a symbol their TPM values are summed (all contributing IDs are listed under the gene name). Protein rows use a representative symbol or a symbol plus UniProt identifier for secondary features. Unmatched features and unmeasured assays are labelled explicitly.
  • One library/replicate per line. These are single measurements; a few lines carry subtype ‡ label conflicts (flagged when shown). Values are rounded for display.
