## C13: interpreting the PC1 decomposition

PC1 is the strongest axis of RNA variation across the models and captures 20.6% of the variation in the analysed expression matrix. We then ask how much of the variation in models' scores along that axis can be described by histotype and centre. These are two successive questions with different denominators.

| Component of PC1-score variation | Approximate fraction | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Unique to histotype | 42.4% | Additional variation explained by adding histotype to a model that already includes centre. |
| Unique to centre | 0.2% | Additional variation explained by adding centre to a model that already includes histotype. |
| Shared | 31.0% | Variation that either factor can explain when considered alone, which cannot be assigned uniquely because histotype and centre are associated. |
| Unexplained by these factors | 26.4% | Variation in PC1 scores left after including both factors. |

Thus histotype alone explains about 73.4% of PC1-score variation, centre alone about 31.2%, and the two together about 73.6%; the shared 31.0% is not added twice. The small unique-centre component does not establish that culture or processing had negligible effects. For example, all high-grade serous RNA models came from one centre, so a centre-associated effect aligned with that histotype contributes to the overlapping component. This is a descriptive partition of associations, not a causal separation of biology from processing. The one-model-per-patient analysis repeats the calculation after refitting PCA and addresses related sublines, but retains the histotype–centre overlap.
