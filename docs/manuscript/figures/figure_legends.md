# OvCAN figure legends

Legends for the September 2026 figure redesign. Figures 1–4 accompany the current Data Descriptor draft; Figures 5–6 and S1–S8 are additional analysis figures. The corresponding PDF pages contain the artwork alone.

## Figure 1. Resource composition and data generation

**A**, Newly generated RNA sequencing and tandem mass tag proteomics, and reprocessed whole-exome sequencing, from 42 ovarian cancer cell models representing 34 patients. Counts indicate models with each assay. **B**, Numbers of models and independent patients in each recorded histotype group; bar lengths indicate model counts. **C**, Model-level assay coverage, split into high-grade serous models on the left and the other histotype groups on the right. Filled slate cells indicate an available profile, and outlined cells indicate that the assay is not included for that model. A single exome column represents both processed variant and copy-number records, which cover the same 23 models. Thirteen models have all three assays. The coloured strip identifies the recorded histotype, using the key in B; brackets label the five patients represented by multiple models. Historical histotype labels are retained. HGS, high-grade serous; CC, clear cell; MC, mucinous; EC, endometrioid; MMMT, carcinosarcoma; SCCOHT, small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcaemic type; LGS, low-grade serous.

## Figure 2. RNA library quality, protein coverage and measurement precision

**a,b**, Each point represents one of 31 RNA-profiled models; shapes and fills indicate the contributing centre, and large hollow symbols show centre medians. The number of genes detected at an estimated count of at least one is shown against pseudoalignment percentage (**a**) and processed fragments (**b**). Detection counts refer to the 22,678 genes retained by the global filter of at least ten estimated counts in at least two models. The shared vertical scale is truncated. **c**, Cross-model interquartile ranges (IQRs) for 8,035 genes measured in at least ten paired models. RNA uses log2(TPM + 1), and protein uses the supplied log2 abundance values. Violins show the distributions; boxes span the IQR, central lines mark medians, and whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 IQR. Numbers give medians. The view ends just above the pooled 99th percentile. Differences between these assay scales are descriptive and do not estimate a causal TMT compression factor. **d**, Counts of the 8,427 protein features by the number of plexes with quantified model channels, plotted on a logarithmic horizontal axis. The rust point denotes 70 features without measurements in any model channel; 6,855 features are quantified in all five plexes. **e**, Reported CV medians and IQRs (slate line and band) and bridge-derived per-measurement CV estimates (rust) within abundance deciles, from lowest to highest. Bridge estimates use SD of paired differences divided by the square root of two, followed by the lognormal CV conversion, under an equal, independent-error approximation. **f**, Primary-minus-bridge log2 abundance differences versus the channel mean for four adjacent-plex links. Solid rust lines mark mean differences; dashed lines mark the mean plus or minus 1.96 SD of paired differences. Each facet reports the number of paired protein measurements. The four links use different biological samples; the asterisk identifies VOA3993, an external proteomics-only bridge sample. The vertical display limit of plus or minus 1.6 log2 units excludes 0.191% of plotted observations; all measurements enter the agreement statistics.

## Figure 3. Expression structure, cross-assay concordance and histotype-associated markers

**a**, Principal component analysis of the 2,000 most variable variance-stabilized RNA genes across 31 models. Features were centred without scaling; axes report explained variance. Point fills indicate annotated histotype and shapes indicate contributing centre. The two models annotated as endometrioid are labelled. Their placement provides expression context and does not establish histological reclassification. **b**, Raw PC1 commonality components for all 31 models and for one representative from each of 28 patients. Variable-gene selection and PCA were repeated within the representative set. Unique histotype and centre components are the increments in R² after adding each factor to the other; shared R² equals the sum of the separate-model R² values minus the joint-model R². Unexplained variance is one minus joint R². Labels show percentages. These summarize different PC1 fits and do not separate causal biological and centre effects; the source and histotype design remains partly aligned. **c**, Distribution of gene-wise Spearman correlations between RNA and protein abundance across the 30 models with both assays. The 8,033 eligible genes have at least ten paired measurements and nonzero variance in both assays. The histogram and smoothed density are descriptive; the rust line marks the median, 0.400. Related sublines, histotype and assay structure remain in these model-level correlations. **d**, Expression of 25 histotype markers and the MKI67 proliferation control, using one RNA-profiled model per patient. Within each marker, means of log2(TPM + 1) were calculated for each histotype and standardized across the six histotype means. Column labels give patient counts. Solid boxes identify the intended high-expression histotype; dashed boxes and downward arrows identify markers expected to be lower in the intended histotype. MKI67 has no intended histotype. **e**, Absolute log2(TPM + 1) values for the same markers and row order across all 31 models. Coloured points represent models of the marker's intended histotype; pale points represent other models. Slight vertical jitter reduces overlap and does not alter the expression coordinate. Horizontal separators identify marker groups. The dashed line at one indicates the expression threshold used for high-expression marker recovery; recovery is assessed from histotype means, not from individual points. Model-level displays preserve within-patient and between-model variation; patient representatives are used for the primary marker comparisons, and small histotype groups limit interpretation. Fifteen of 25 marker checks were recovered in the patient-representative assessment, compared with a mean of 8.46 under 20,000 joint label permutations within centre (P = 0.0101); MKI67 was excluded.

## Figure 4. Variant curation, external expression matching and copy-number profiles

**A**, Variant counts before and after filtering, shown for all 23 models with whole-exome sequencing (WES) and for OV2295 as an example. The logarithmic count axis shows all Mutect2 records, calls passing FilterMutectCalls, and coding nonsynonymous candidates retained after the specified population-frequency filter (maximum annotated frequency ≤0.001). Across the 23 models, the corresponding counts are 582,474, 16,081 and 6,194; OV2295 contributes 25,914, 493 and 170. These are tumour-only candidates, not confirmed somatic mutations.

**B**, Expression matching for the five models shared with DepMap Public 24Q4. Correlations use 2,000 variable genes and all 67 DepMap ovarian models. Grey ticks show the 66 non-self correlations per project model; the open rust circle marks the best non-self match and the filled circle the self-match. Each self-match ranks first among the 67 comparisons and is the reciprocal best match among the project RNA models. BIN67 and COV434 are each other's closest non-self matches. These comparisons support identity for this overlapping subset and do not authenticate every model or replace current-stock STR testing.

**C**, Canonical-driver candidates in 23 models from 16 patients. Columns are grouped by histotype and patient. HGS, high-grade serous; CC, clear cell; EC, endometrioid; LGS, low-grade serous; MC, mucinous. These are the recorded histotype annotations; discordant evidence for individual models is discussed separately. Brackets identify the four patient groups with multiple WES-profiled models in this panel. The upper track shows each model's coding-candidate count on a logarithmic scale, with a lower reference of 100. Each gene/model marker combines variant class (square, truncating; circle, missense; triangle, in-frame indel; diamond, multiple variant classes) with the best priority tier present in that cell. Tier 1 is the strongest prioritization category, followed by Tiers 2 and 3; the tiers do not establish somatic origin. Pale empty cells indicate no retained candidate in the displayed gene. The right-hand bars and labels count patients with at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 candidate, using the union across their derived models and a denominator of 16. Tier 3 candidates remain visible but are excluded from these patient counts. Genes are ordered by the Tier 1–2 patient count. TP53 candidates are retained in all 18 HGSC models, representing 11 patients.

**D**, Gain and loss frequencies across 39 assessed autosomal arms in the 11 HGSC patients. Gains extend upward and losses downward; axis labels show absolute percentages. A model-level arm is called altered when more than half of the full non-centromeric arm length has centered log2 copy ratio above 0.20 or below −0.20. Segments are intersected with hg38 cytoband-defined arms, excluding centromeric intervals. Acrocentric p arms 13p, 14p, 15p, 21p and 22p are not assessed. Patient calls are the union across derived models, so a patient can contribute to both directions if different derived models meet different criteria. CNVkit log2 ratios are centered by the autosomal probe-weighted median. These relative ratios do not determine absolute ploidy, loss of heterozygosity or homologous-recombination deficiency.

## Figure 5. Exploratory mutation spectra and selected model-specific molecular profiles

**A**, Coding-candidate counts across all 23 WES-profiled models, ordered by count. The horizontal count axis is logarithmic; point colour indicates the proportion of coding SNVs and indels that are indels. The dashed line marks the median of 206 candidates. TOV21G has 1,416 coding candidates, approximately 6.9 times the median. This within-panel outlier is not a calibrated tumour mutational burden measurement.

**B**, The SBS-96 spectrum of TOV21G, derived from 2,417 exome-wide SNVs passing the caller and population-frequency filters. Channels are grouped by the six pyrimidine-centered substitution classes and ordered by the 5′ and 3′ flanking bases within each class. Bar heights are fractions of the model's SNVs. The spectrum uses a broader candidate set than the coding-only counts in A and the GRCh38 reference sequence.

**C**, Maximum cosine similarity between the TOV21G spectrum and the prespecified mismatch-repair-associated, clock-like and POLE-proofreading-associated groups of COSMIC v3.2 SBS signatures. The displayed labels identify each group's strongest match: SBS6, 0.877; SBS5, 0.651; and SBS10c, 0.315. These are descriptive similarities without a diagnostic cutoff; similarity to a reference signature alone does not establish a mutational process or MSI status.

**D**, Sensitivity of the TOV21G MMR-associated fitted fraction to the reference setting. All 2,417 SNVs overlap the recovered CNVkit target footprint. “Genome / full” uses the genome-based reference with the full signature set; “Target / full” adjusts reference trinucleotide opportunities to that target footprint; “Target / restricted” uses the adjusted reference and the prespecified restricted signature set. The resulting fractions are 0.733, 0.447 and 0.475. These are exploratory point estimates, with no confidence intervals shown. The material change after opportunity adjustment and the unresolved somatic/germline mixture limit biological interpretation. MSI status remains unconfirmed.

**E**, RNA and protein expression of selected lineage markers in the three models annotated as mucinous. Markers comprise KRT7, PAX8 and WT1; intestinal-associated SATB2, CDX2, KRT20 and MUC2; and gastric-associated MUC5AC, TFF1 and TFF3. Each gene is standardized across all 31 models measured in its assay, rather than across only the three displayed models. Grey cells with crosses indicate markers not measured in that assay. Expression patterns are descriptive and do not independently determine ovarian versus gastrointestinal origin.

**F**, Selected SWI/SNF-related molecular evidence. The six models satisfying the exploratory subunit-loss rules are shown together with OV2295, whose Tier-3-only truncating candidate is not admitted as loss evidence. Fill shows the within-assay gene z score; numbers give abundance rank among the 31 models in that assay, with 1 indicating the lowest value. Compact WES labels give the affected gene and its best priority tier. An asterisk marks a truncating candidate excluded from the operational loss rule because its only support is Tier 3. An empty WES label is not a claim of wild-type status or absence of WES. In particular, BIN67 has SMARCA4 RNA rank 23/31 and protein rank 2/31, illustrating RNA/protein discordance. Relative abundance and these exploratory rules do not establish complete protein loss or a histopathological reclassification. E and F share a slate–white–rust z-score scale displayed with saturation at ±3; untruncated values remain in the source tables.

## Figure 6. Descriptive atlas of antibody–drug conjugate target abundance

**A**, RNA and protein abundance for nine target genes across the same 30 models with both modalities: 15 HGS, six CC, two EC, three MC, two MMMT and two SCCOHT models. Rows are standardised separately within each gene and modality across these models. Colours show z scores, with values outside −3 to 3 assigned the end colours. Numbers to the right are the corresponding cross-model interquartile ranges (IQRs) in the original log2 expression units. Crosses denote protein targets without quantified values; DPEP3 protein is unquantified in all displayed models. DPEP3 RNA has an IQR of zero, illustrating that row scaling can emphasise small absolute differences. RNA units are log2(TPM + 1); protein units are the supplied log2 abundances normalised using the pooled internal standard. **B**, Individual FOLR1 abundances in 15 HGS models from 12 patients, with vertical jitter to separate points. The RNA bimodality coefficient is 0.440; an equal-variance Gaussian-mixture comparison favours one component (two-minus-one-component BIC difference, −1.06). The displayed range does not establish bimodality. **C**, Protein-to-RNA ratios of the cross-model range, IQR and standard deviation for the eight targets quantified in both assays, using the same 30 paired models for each comparison. These descriptive differences in spread do not distinguish biological from measurement contributions. The broader assay-specific data contain 31 RNA and 31 protein models; VOA6861 is RNA-only and VOA14993 is protein-only, so neither enters this paired atlas. Bulk abundance does not establish cell-surface localisation, target accessibility or drug response. Target names are FRα (FOLR1), mesothelin (MSLN), HER2 (ERBB2), NaPi2b (SLC34A2), TROP2 (TACSTD2), CDH6, B7-H3 (CD276), B7-H4 (VTCN1) and DPEP3. Histotype abbreviations follow Figure 1.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Proteomic ordination and histotype separation

**A, B.** Principal components of the 2,000 most variable complete-case protein features across 31 models, using the supplied normalized log2 abundances, centred without feature scaling. The same coordinates are coloured by annotated histotype (A) and TMT plex (B). PC1 and PC2 explain 12.7% and 9.4% of variance, respectively. The clear-cell model VOA10816 is labelled at the PC2 extreme. Visual overlap does not establish the absence of batch effects; plex and contributing centre should be considered in comparisons.

**C.** Mean silhouette widths for the annotated histotypes in RNA and protein data, using Euclidean distances on the first ten principal components of each modality. Lines connect summaries for the same histotype. Values near zero indicate overlapping groups; negative values indicate greater similarity to another group. Parentheses give model counts, and All denotes the overall model-weighted summary. These descriptive model-level estimates include related sublines; they are not independent-patient estimates. Histotype abbreviations: HGS, high-grade serous; CC, clear cell; EC, historical endometrioid; MC, mucinous; MMMT, malignant mixed Müllerian tumour; SCCOHT, small cell carcinoma of the ovary, hypercalcaemic type.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Passage associations and cross-assay passage differences

**A.** Associations between recorded passage number and the first five RNA principal components in 31 models, including related sublines. Light grey bars show R² for passage alone; slate and rust bars show the increment in total R² after adding passage to models containing centre, or histotype and centre, respectively. The conditional measures are ΔR², not partial R² rescaled by residual variance. Passage, centre and histotype are partly aligned; these descriptive associations do not estimate a causal effect of passage.

**B.** Paired RNA-seq and WES passage records for the 13 models with both values, ordered by RNA-seq passage. Connecting segments identify the two records from the same model; other models lack one or both values. WES minus RNA-seq passage ranges from -17 to +20, with a median absolute difference of 4 passages. The descriptive Pearson correlation is -0.33. Protein passage records are not represented.

## Supplementary Figure S5. Genome-wide relative copy-number profiles

Rows show all 23 WES-profiled models; columns show consecutive 10-Mb bins across chromosomes 1–22 in GRCh38 (300 bins, with shorter terminal bins). Each displayed value is the segment-overlap-length-weighted mean of the centered CNVkit log2 copy ratios intersecting that bin. Histotype labels identify the row groups. The patient-family strip identifies related models using the same family identifiers as the main figures; “Other” denotes models outside those named multi-model families. The heatmap has no row or column clustering.

The slate–white–rust scale is centered at zero and saturates at ±1.5. Grey cells indicate no segment overlap, not absence of WES or demonstrated absence of exome coverage. The right-hand bars show fraction of genome altered (FGA), calculated from the underlying autosomal segments rather than from the display bins: the fraction of represented autosomal segment length with absolute centered log2 ratio above 0.20. TOV81D has the lowest FGA (0.021). Profiles are centered by the autosomal probe-weighted median; sex chromosomes are excluded. The external reference normals used the same capture kit according to the author. Relative profiles and FGA do not establish absolute ploidy, allele-specific copy number or loss of heterozygosity. The coarser display bins should not be used to infer individual-gene copy status.

## Supplementary Figure S6. Exploratory classifier outputs and RNA pathway-score clusters

**A**, Uncalibrated class probabilities from consensusOV 1.30.0 for the 15 HGSC RNA-profiled models. Columns show differentiated (DIF), proliferative (PRO), immunoreactive (IMR) and mesenchymal (MES) classes. Cell labels give the probabilities rounded to two decimals; an outline identifies the highest-probability class. The model ordering follows this class and the top-minus-second probability margin. The asterisk marks OV2085, whose assignment changes from IMR to PRO when the classifier is applied using the broader RNA-model input set instead of the HGSC-only set.

**B**, Top-minus-second probability margins, with rows aligned to A. The pale shaded region marks margins below 0.10 (six models); the dashed line marks the median margin of 0.158. These are descriptive summaries of classifier ambiguity, not calibrated confidence or significance thresholds.

**C**, Illustrative k=3 Ward.D2 clusters from the HGSC Hallmark pathway scores, using the same model ordering. Cluster 1 is the hypoxic/glycolytic group, cluster 2 the low-signalling group and cluster 3 the inflammatory/NF-κB/EMT group. These names summarize relative pathway patterns within this panel. Assignments change when one model per patient is selected and the clustering is repeated (15 models, 12 patients; adjusted Rand index 0.189 on shared representatives). Bulk-tumour classifier classes can reflect microenvironmental contributions and are not validated histotypes for these cultures. Neither the classifier outputs nor these unstable RNA clusters establish model identity or new biological subtypes.

## Supplementary Figure S7. Patient-level marker effect sizes and uncertainty

Twenty-five prespecified marker checks across 28 patient representatives, using symbol-summed log2(TPM + 1) RNA abundance. MKI67 is an additional display control in the main figure and is excluded here. **A.** Signed Cohen's d for the intended histotype versus the remaining representatives. Negative effects are expected for markers annotated for low abundance (↓). **B.** AUC oriented to the expected direction: values above 0.5 indicate higher abundance for high markers or lower abundance for low markers in the intended histotype. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 stratified bootstrap resamples. Large intervals reflect small groups; intervals for groups of two or three patients are poorly calibrated. Upper Cohen's d interval bounds beyond the displayed maximum of 6 are shown by arrow labels with their actual values; no point estimates are omitted. Brackets give the number of intended-histotype patient representatives.

Circles mark recovery under the prespecified rank rule applied to histotype means in these same patient representatives: the intended histotype must rank among the top two for high markers, with mean log2(TPM + 1) above 1, or among the bottom two for low markers. Triangles mark checks outside that rule. An asterisk identifies BH-adjusted Wilcoxon q < 0.05 across the 25 markers (4 markers: SPP1, HNF1B, KRT20, SMARCA2). A dagger identifies a rank-sum comparison constrained by the minimum attainable p value (SMARCA2). Individual marker comparisons are not adjusted for centre; these intervals and tests should not be interpreted as isolating histotype from centre effects. The panel-wide centre-restricted recovery permutation analysis is a separate analysis described in the main text. Histotype abbreviations are defined in Supplementary Figure S1.

## Supplementary Figure S8. Illustrative within-HGS expression patterns

**A.** Inflammatory and hypoxic-glycolytic theme scores for 15 HGS models from 12 patients. Colours identify an illustrative k = 3 Ward.D2 partition of all 50 Hallmark gene-set scores standardized within HGS. The groups therefore should not be treated as stable molecular subtypes. **B.** Signed proliferation theme scores for the same models, ordered by value; zero denotes the mean within HGS. **C.** PROGENy scores for 14 pathways standardized within HGS.
